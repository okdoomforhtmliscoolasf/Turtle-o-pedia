CHAPTER 04 / SHARED FUTURES

Ancient lineage.
Modern pressures.

Turtles have persisted through vast changes in Earth’s history. Today, many species face rapid pressures from human activity and changing habitats.

CONSERVATIONTHREATS / RESPONSES

A GROUP UNDER PRESSURE

Long lives.
Slow replacement.

Many turtles mature slowly and produce young that face high mortality early in life. When adults are removed faster than populations can replace them, recovery can take a long time.

Threat levels differ between species and populations. A global label cannot describe every local population equally well, so conservation depends on reliable monitoring and local context.

01

Fishing bycatch

Turtles can become trapped in fishing gear and may be unable to surface to breathe. Gear modifications and turtle-excluder devices can reduce accidental capture.

02

Coastal development

Construction and artificial lighting can disturb nesting beaches and disorient hatchlings moving toward the sea.

03

Plastic & marine debris

Floating plastic can be mistaken for prey, while discarded fishing gear can entangle animals.

04

Illegal trade & harvesting

Illegal collection of eggs, meat and shells has affected turtle populations in many regions.

05

Climate pressures

Warming conditions and changes to nesting beaches can affect incubation environments and coastal habitats.

06

Habitat degradation

Damage to wetlands, rivers, seagrass beds, reefs and nesting beaches reduces places turtles need to feed and reproduce.

SMALL ACTIONS / REAL IMPACT

Protect the places
they depend on.

  • Keep nesting beaches dark and respect marked nesting areas.
  • Never buy turtle shells, eggs or products made from protected wildlife.
  • Reduce single-use plastics and dispose of fishing line safely.
  • Observe wild turtles from a respectful distance; never chase or touch them.
  • Support credible local conservation and rescue organisations.

A NOTE FOR INDIA

Coasts, rivers & wetlands matter.

India supports important turtle habitats, including nesting beaches used by olive ridley sea turtles along the Odisha coast. Local conservation work combines habitat protection, community participation and safer fishing practices.
