CHAPTER 02 / FORM & FUNCTION

A body shaped
around its shell.

The shell protects the body, but it also changes how turtles breathe, move and carry their weight.

ANATOMYCARAPACE / PLASTRON

THE TWO SIDES

Top. Bottom.
One connected structure.

The domed upper shell is the carapace. The flatter underside is the plastron. In most turtles, bony bridges connect them along the sides.

SIMPLIFIED EDUCATIONAL DIAGRAM · NOT TO SCALE

SELECTED STRUCTURE

Carapace

The carapace is fused with the turtle’s ribs and vertebrae. It is part of the skeleton, not a detachable container.

BREATHING

How do you breathe
inside a rigid shell?

Turtles cannot expand their rib cage in the same way many other reptiles and mammals do. Instead, specialised muscle groups move internal organs and ventilate the lungs.

Even species that spend much of their time underwater breathe air. They must surface to refill their lungs, though the time they can remain submerged varies by species and conditions.
